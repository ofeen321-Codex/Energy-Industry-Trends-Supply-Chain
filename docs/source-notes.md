# Source assessment and reconciliation notes

Read alongside the [source list](sources.md) and [report](../reports/energy-supply-chain-outlook.md). Research cutoff: October 23, 2026. Notes paraphrase findings; cached full texts are ignored by Git.

## Revision 2: broader evidence and changed interpretation

The current report cites 28 records across 13 publisher/study families. This is a breadth measure, not 28 independent confirmations. All new substantive inputs were opened and read; access-only leads remain separate. The original-source assessments below remain relevant even when a legacy statistic is no longer foregrounded.

| New evidence | Date / scope | Contribution and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| B01 — World Bank Pink Sheet | October 2, 2026; monthly prices through September | Adds observed regional gas benchmarks and commodity context. Europe TTF, U.S. Henry Hub and Japan cif import prices have different definitions; Japan's recent values are estimated. Do not turn their ratio into an industrial electricity-cost ratio. |
| B02 — World Bank commodity outlook release | April 28, 2026 | Adds an independent macro forecast and its explicit recovery assumptions. Its $86 2026 Brent baseline is an older vintage than EIA's October $96, not an equally current consensus estimate. The October Pink Sheet is observed data, not an October outlook forecast. |
| B03/B04 — Berkeley Lab Queued Up | Catalog May 2026; PDF cover June 2026; requests/outcomes through end-2025 | Adds national-lab evidence on transmission-connected generation, completion rates and duration. Covers about 98% of installed U.S. generating capacity; excludes load requests, distribution and behind-the-meter projects. Completed-project median duration is not a facility-load connection promise. Catalog/cover dates are not silently harmonized. |
| B05 — PJM auction release | July 14, 2026; 2028/2029 delivery year | Adds operator evidence of forward planning pressure. UCAP is accredited capacity; $/MW-day is not $/MWh. Delivery is outside this report's horizon, and later interventions may change outcomes. Shortfall does not imply inevitable outages. |
| B06 — BNEF cost release | February 18, 2026; 2025 benchmarks | Commercial modeling of generation and four-hour storage costs; public release only. A solar benchmark and a levelized storage cost differ from IRENA's weighted project costs and installed $/kWh. Do not combine into a firm power price without a project model. |
| B07 — BNEF storage outlook | October 22, 2026 | Newest substantive input: forecast 188 GW/535 GWh in 2026 and 209 GW/638 GWh in 2027. Forecasts are not measured deployments. Older 158/459 estimates in B14 and Ember are retained as older vintages; changes in coverage/assumptions are not fully reconciled. |
| B09/B18 — IRENA original summary and PNNL catalog | July 2026; 2025 project estimates | Adds region-specific generation LCOE. PNNL's BibTeX supports month precision; a displayed July 1 is not treated as a verified exact release day. PNNL catalogs the same IRENA study, not independent cost evidence. Landing page B08 returned 403; original PDF B09 was accessible. |
| B10/B16 — Commerce solar cases | Announcement September 11; India notice September 16, 2026 | Adds country/product/producer-specific trade exposure. A Commerce final determination differs from an ITC injury determination, duty order, cash-deposit instruction and final importer liability. Follow-through must be reverified before pricing an entry. No blanket rate is applied in this report. |
| B11 — processed-mineral proclamation | Signed January 14; published January 20, 2026 | Documents negotiations and possible subsequent remedies, not an automatically imposed uniform mineral tariff. Subsequent action cannot be inferred from the earlier proclamation. |
| B12 — Morgan Lewis contract analysis | March 13, 2026 | Specialist legal commentary on storage supply, EPC/BTA structures, warranties, throughput, changes in law and contractor interfaces. Useful to design questions and contract allocations; not controlling law, verified company failure or a statistical market sample. |
| B15 — USTR HTS/exclusion amendments | September 2 publication; July 1, 2026 effective date | Four conforming exclusion-description amendments. Relevant to classification discipline; no evidence that every battery or energy item receives an exclusion. |
| B17 — BNEF minerals outlook | October 20, 2026 | Adds a newer independent commercial outlook, conditional long-run supply sufficiency, concentration risk and possible 2027 lithium relief. Copper deficit starts in 2028 in this scenario; 2050 quantities are not next-year shortages. IEA/BNEF scenario differences remain visible. |
| B14 — Energy-Storage.news | May 8, 2026 | Credible sector reporting of an older BNEF forecast. Same underlying forecast family, not independent corroboration or the current outlook. |

**Access gaps:** B13 Reuters still returned 401; no article content was assumed. The IRENA landing page and USGS PDF access failures are retained rather than treated as failed source credibility. Some discovery URLs returned 404 and were corrected from original publisher links; retrieval attempts remain in the log.

**Key safeguards for updates:** distinguish source date, data period, forecast horizon, retrieval and editorial review; preserve definitions and methods; maintain both global and U.S. effects; prefer operative primary documents for legal assertions. More source families improve the opportunity to challenge a view but do not guarantee a consensus.

## The nine starting sources

| ID | Contribution | Qualification and use |
|---|---|---|
| S01 — RSM, 2026 edition; exact update date not stated | Six themes: energy demand and mix, policy/capital, AI, cyber resilience, supply chains and workforce | Commercial interpretation, not a statistical forecast. Its assertion of gas as a long-term backbone is an outlook; used for business context and supplier/cyber requirements, not proof of future gas prices. Original article was published in 2025 and later updated. |
| S02 — INVERTO, 2026 edition; exact date not verified | Sovereignty, execution capacity, EPC constraints, AI and procurement capabilities | Supports differentiated category strategies and early engineering/procurement coordination. Broad cost and capacity statistics often refer to external analyses. Its >1,000 TWh data-center projection for 2026 is not adopted: the later Ember review cites a materially lower IEA trajectory (about 500 TWh in 2025 and 950 TWh in 2030), with different vintages/possible scope. Broad equipment-cost pressure is not generalized to solar modules and battery packs. |
| S03 — Ember, April 21, 2026; web metadata modified June 22 | Global 2025 demand +849 TWh; solar +636; wind +205; U.S. demand +131 and solar +85 | Principal annual electricity snapshot. Annual source contributions are not hourly matching. The publisher is a transition-focused think tank. |
| S04 — Ember PDF, April 21, 2026 | Cross-check of global-review findings and methodology; 215-country coverage, with 2025 data for 91 countries representing 93% of demand | Same study as S03, not independent corroboration. Some country values are estimated; compare AC/DC capacity carefully. Its estimated global 2025 battery deployment of 250 GWh differs from S06's later 307 GWh estimate. No harmonized deployment growth chart is constructed. |
| S05 — Ember, January 16, 2026 | Early U.S. estimate: solar +83 TWh, demand +135 TWh; 61% of growth | Different from S03's +85/+131 or 65%. Use the later review for common-vintage annual comparisons; do not claim the difference is fully explained or use either as solar's total share. Its provisional battery power-capacity number is not used to resolve different storage metrics in other reports. |
| S06 — Ember, August 12, 2026; metadata modified August 19 | Solar's daytime concentration, storage expansion, observed evening contribution in selected markets | The 34% shifting figure is a conditional theoretical ceiling for new daily solar, based on expected battery additions and H1 solar growth. Not actual global battery output. Global installed-cost estimates are not current U.S. vendor quotes. The methodology includes reconstructed hourly profiles and estimated battery contributions in some markets. |
| S07 — Ember, July 16, 2026 | China storage growth, revenue stacking, co-location/standalone utilization gap and market reforms | 199 and 299 cycles are reported 2025 utilization estimates; 350 is a modeled optimization benchmark. Revenue stacking depends on rules and may not permit concurrent double compensation. Chinese operating results are not automatically U.S. returns. |
| S08 — Ember, April 14, 2026 | Energy-security case for electrification after the twin fossil shocks | Strong advocacy and long-run extrapolation. Its claims of irreversible transition or fossil-demand peaking are not adopted as established next-year facts. October EIA/IEA reporting updates the physical fuel-market situation. |
| S09 — RMI, May 8, 2026; metadata modified June 26 | U.S. grid materials, equipment, workforce, customization and qualification constraints; policy opportunities | Up-to-four-year lead time is not a universal quote. Domestic-demand coverage is a reported estimate, not an unfilled-demand measure. Supply gaps through 2030 are a projection. Proposed policy remedies are not assumed enacted. |

## Discovery and unavailable material

- D02–D10 and D13–D15 are mostly navigation or monitoring sources, not independently verified evidence for a numerical finding. D04 returned a sparse assessments shell, insufficient for a reserve-margin claim. A13 was inspected as a regulatory lead but no order-specific outcome is asserted in the report.
- USGS A16 confirms the 2026 publication record (February 6, 2026) and links a September 18 data release. A07/A08 on `pubs.usgs.gov` returned 403. The underlying mineral data were not read and are not cited as statistics. The IEA July outlook provides the report's mineral evidence.
- Reuters D12 returned 401; CBP D11 returned 403. No unread reporting or legal guidance is treated as corroboration.
- Initial Bing RSS results were largely irrelevant to the complete queries. DuckDuckGo returned useful EIA discovery for early searches, but later queries yielded no usable links; those are not successful literature coverage. Direct EIA and IEA indexes supplied the latest articles opened and used.
- Exact dates for RSM and INVERTO were not verified. Copyright years, URL years, and dates in related-article cards are not publication dates.
- Cross-source independence matters: S03/S04, D01/A01–A03, A14/A19 and A15/A20 are paired publications/sections, not independent studies.
